# Communicating with Slip Boxes (raw source stub)

*Synthetic Tier-B raw text for the demo RAG layer. Not a real transcription of any work; written to give the retrieval demo plausible source content.*

The slip-box is not a container into which notes are simply deposited and later retrieved. It is better understood as a partner in communication. When a note is written, it is given a fixed address and then connected to whatever existing notes it bears on, regardless of subject area. Over years, these connections accumulate into a network whose structure no single act of filing ever designed.

The decisive consequence is that the slip-box can produce combinations the author never deliberately stored. Following a chain of links during writing, one arrives at notes assembled long ago in a different context, and their juxtaposition suggests arguments that were not anticipated. In this sense the box answers questions that were never explicitly asked of it.

Two design rules make this possible. First, each note should hold a single, self-contained idea, written so that it remains intelligible when read out of its original context. Second, organisation must be by reference rather than by category: a note earns its place through the links pointing to and from it, not through membership in a predetermined hierarchy.

Because the network grows from the bottom up, its usefulness scales with its connectedness rather than its size. A small, densely linked collection is more productive than a large, sparsely linked one. The work of note-taking, on this view, is mostly the work of linking — deciding what a new idea connects to is itself the intellectual labour the method is designed to provoke.
