# Communicating with Slip Boxes

Niklas Luhmann's short essay describes the slip-box (Zettelkasten) he used as a productivity and thinking partner across his prolific career. It is the canonical primary source for the [[zettelkasten]] method.

> **Paper claim** — Luhmann frames the slip-box as a communication partner: by linking atomic notes, the collection produces combinations the author did not consciously file, surfacing them during writing.

## Key points

- Notes are atomic and addressed by stable numbers, allowing indefinite branching.
- Value comes from links between notes, not from a fixed classification scheme.
- The slip-box's "surprises" arise from emergent adjacencies in the link graph.
