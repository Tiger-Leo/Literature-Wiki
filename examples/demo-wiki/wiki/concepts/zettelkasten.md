# Zettelkasten

The **Zettelkasten** (German for "slip-box") is a method of personal knowledge management built from atomic notes, each capturing a single idea and connected to others by explicit links. Rather than filing notes under fixed categories, the method emphasises a dense web of cross-references, so that structure emerges from the connections rather than from a predetermined hierarchy.

## Atomic notes and linking

The core unit is the atomic note: one idea, written in the author's own words, addressable by a stable identifier. Notes accrue value as they are linked, and the growing link graph lets unexpected adjacencies surface during writing. The sociologist Niklas Luhmann's working method is the canonical reference point ([[luhmann-1992]]).

## Emergent structure

Because organisation is bottom-up, a mature slip-box behaves less like an archive and more like a conversation partner — Luhmann described querying his slip-box and being surprised by what it returned.

## Relation to review

A Zettelkasten captures and connects knowledge but does not by itself schedule its rehearsal; pairing durable notes with [[spaced-repetition]] closes that gap, turning the collection into both a thinking tool and a study aid.
