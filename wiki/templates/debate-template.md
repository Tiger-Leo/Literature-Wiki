# Debate Title

One encyclopedic paragraph (4–7 sentences) that states the question, names the positions, says why the disagreement matters, and previews the page.

## The Question

Precise statement of what is being debated. Distinguish it from neighboring questions that are sometimes conflated with it.

## Position A — <short label>

Integrated treatment of the position: its claim, its theoretical or empirical grounding, and the papers that have advanced or refined it. Multi-cite where multiple papers contribute to the same point; name papers individually only where their distinct contribution matters to the position.

## Position B — <short label>

Same shape as Position A.

(If there is a Position C, add it. Keep the count small — most genuine debates have two or three positions, not seven.)

## Where the Disagreement Actually Sits

The crux of the debate. Often the positions agree on more than they disagree on, and the disagreement is concentrated in a specific assumption, identification strategy, or interpretive move. Make that crux visible.

## Empirical Anchors

The specific empirical evidence that bears on the debate. Organise by what each piece of evidence rules in or out, not by which paper produced it.

## Attempts at Unification

Papers that have tried to reconcile the positions or that show the positions are special cases of a more general framework. Cite inline.

## Frontier / Extension *(only if the round plan declares this in scope)*

How the debate is being recast in a frontier sub-literature (e.g., AI-era, post-replication, behavioural). Omit if no such sub-literature.

## Current State of the Debate

> **Current assessment (YYYY-MM):** Where the wiki currently stands — open, partially resolved, or resolved. State what evidence would change the assessment.

3–6 bullets.
