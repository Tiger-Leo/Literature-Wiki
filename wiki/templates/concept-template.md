# Concept Name

One encyclopedic paragraph (4–7 sentences) that defines the concept, says why it matters in the literature, and previews the structure of the page. No bullets. No single-paper-only citations. Inline `[[slug]]` only where genuinely necessary to anchor a foundational claim.

## Definition and Scope

What the concept is and what it is not. Standard operationalization in the field. Cite the founding paper(s) inline: "[[founding-paper]] introduced X as ..." — but the paragraph is about the concept, not about the paper.

## <Sub-topic or sub-question 1>

Integrated prose drawing on multiple papers. Where several papers say the same thing, fold them into one sentence with multi-citation: "Across the field-experiment literature, X holds robustly ([[slug-a]]; [[slug-b]]; [[slug-c]])."

> **Current assessment (YYYY-MM):** The wiki's current view on this sub-topic, with date. Use sparingly — once per section at most.

## <Sub-topic or sub-question 2>

Same shape — prose organised around the sub-topic, multi-citation where claims converge, inline naming where they diverge.

## Formal Model *(if applicable)*

For formal-theory concepts: state the model. Variables, action spaces, equilibrium concept, key result. Use `$...$` for inline math and `$$...$$` for block math. Cite the paper(s) that introduced the formalism inline.

## Empirical Synthesis

Cross-paper pattern across the empirical literature. Organise by sub-finding, not by paper. *"Across N studies in M settings, ..."* Use italics for cross-paper claims; multi-cite in parentheses.

## Tensions and Refinements

Where the concept has been contested, refined, or unified with adjacent concepts. Name the disagreement and the papers in sentence flow; do not split into "Position A" and "Position B" sub-sections (that's for debate pages).

## Frontier / Extension *(only if the round plan declares this concept in scope for an extension axis)*

The extension or frontier reading of the concept — e.g., AI-era reformulation, behavioural turn, new methodological wave. Omit this section entirely if the corpus has no such sub-literature.

## Open Questions

What remains unresolved. 3–6 bullet points, each one sentence.
