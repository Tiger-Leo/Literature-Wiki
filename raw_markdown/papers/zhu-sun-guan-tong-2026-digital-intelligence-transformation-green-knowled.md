Purpose – Standard models of directed technical change treat relative prices and regulation as the main forces steering innovation toward cleaner technologies. From a knowledge management perspective, this paper aims to examine whether digital-intelligence transformation can tilt innovation by reshaping knowledge system inside the firm. Design/methodology/approach – This paper tests these predictions in a panel of Chinese listed firms, proxying innovation direction with the green patent share and its year-to-year adjustment. Findings – A one−standard deviation increase in digital-intelligence transformation the green patent share by about 7.4% of its sample mean. Decomposing the mechanism, this paper finds that digital intelligence strengthens both exploratory and exploitative recombination, but only exploration translates into a greener direction, whereas exploitation reinforces lock-in, leaving a positive net effect. The shift is most pronounced where environmental regulation is more stringent. It is also concentrated among firms that lack alternative offline search networks, and it is strongest at intermediate levels of knowledge breadth, with excessive breadth diluting depth and attenuating the marginal gain. Originality/value – This paper argues that digital-intelligence transformation can lower the perceived cost of sourcing codifiable external knowledge while making system integration and tacit coordination more salient constraints, so the direction of innovation reflects a tug-of-war between exploratory recombination and exploitative lock-in. These results suggest a knowledge-management-based nonprice micro-channel through which digital-intelligence transformation shapes directed technical change. Keywords Digital-intelligence transformation, Directed technical change, Knowledge recombination, Exploratory recombination, Exploitative recombination

# Digital intelligence transformation, green knowledge recombination capability and directed technical change in firms

Shuai Zhu, Zhaoyang Sun, Wei Guan and Peishan Tong

## Abstract

Paper type Research paper

## 1. Introduction

The green transition is not only an external compliance challenge but also a knowledge management (KM) challenge inside the firm. Enterprises not only have to make commitments but also need to ensure that these commitments can be verified in terms of process and source. The longer the supply chain and the more fragmented the division of labor, the more difficult it is for enterprises to identify the true sources of key inputs, and compliance disclosure will fail at the point of information breakage (Kim and Davis, 2016). The establishment of chain governance depends on whether the rules can be embedded in transaction relationships and whether auditing and accountability can enter the organizational process (van Tulder et al., 2009; Miles and Munilla, 2004). Thus, green transition depends on firms’ ability to organize traceable, transferable and accountable knowledge across organizational boundaries.

When digital resources are scalable, the scale and scope of enterprises present a new combination, being both more focused and more dependent on external collaboration (Giustiziero et al., 2023). Therefore, the green transition does not occur within a static boundary but happens in the process of organizational and knowledge boundaries becoming more permeable.

The thinning of boundaries first lowers the cost of external acquisition. Enterprise systems and communication technologies strip more service processes away from face-to-face contact, making them more amenable to transactional and offshoreization (Stratman, 2008); IT investment and process innovation are intertwined, and their returns show systematic differences among enterprises (Tambe and Hitt, 2012). Outsourcing can also bring observable efficiency gains, especially when service providers have a stronger knowledge stock (Chang and Gurbaxani, 2012), and outsourcing reduces operational costs often relying on internal complementary investments for monitoring and coordination (Han and Mithas, 2013). External search becomes more convenient, and information and modules are more easily incorporated into the organizational boundary. In KM terms, digital interfaces expand the pool of searchable and codifiable knowledge, but they do not automatically solve the problem of translating such knowledge into firm-specific routines (Li et al., 2023).

In offshore projects, clarifying requirements, knowledge transfer, control and coordination constitute the main additional costs, and the higher the proportion of customer-specific knowledge, the higher the cost (Dibbern et al., 2008). Contract design must make trade-offs between complexity, audit efficiency and incentive compatibility (Dey et al., 2010), and the outsourcing structure itself may also cause integrated outsourcing to deviate from the optimal of the principal (Lee et al., 2012). Relationship learning depends on the matching of coordination mechanisms and cooperation motivations (Im and Ahuja, 2023), and crossboundary operation depends on a sustainable trust structure (Zou et al., 2023). Therefore, enterprises more often outsource general skills tasks and keep enterprise-specific parts within Espinosa (2021). When control rights and knowledge rights are separated among different entities, the reverse entry of subcontractors will shift outsourcing from an efficiency issue to a competition issue (Arrun˜ada and Va´zquez, 2006). In industries with stronger system integration pressure, modular outsourcing and hierarchical control alternate more frequently as a norm, because quality guarantees and system integration are difficult to be fully replaced by market contracts (Jacobides et al., 2016). Green innovation brings this group of issues to the forefront. Green processes often integrate into equipment parameters, on-site constraints and multi-party collaboration chains, with high hidden components and more ambiguous measurement boundaries. In the green building materials market, enterprises can quickly search for channel partners through platforms, outsource standardized materials and break down the encodable parts into contracts; however, to make end-users accept the green premium, it is necessary to form a stable collaborative order through local trust, policy interpretation and multi-party negotiation. This part of knowledge is difficult to compress into a tradable interface. The transfer of tacit knowledge resists digital interfaces; it is subject to spatial interaction and is also constrained by attention accumulation and cognitive stickiness within the organization (Bai et al., 2024; Nayak et al., 2025; Argote et al., 2022; Ringov et al., 2026; Tafkov et al., 2022; Pham et al., 2025). Digital-intelligence transformation may lower the acquisition cost of external codifiable knowledge, but it also makes tacit process collaboration and system integration more salient constraints in green knowledge recombination.

These bottlenecks pose a direct challenge to directional technological change. The classic model bases the direction shift on relative returns and substitution elasticity, and policies can lead innovation toward the clean end (Acemoglu et al., 2012). The path dependence model emphasizes that the direction of innovation will self-reinforce, and price tools can partially unlock it (Aghion et al., 2016). These theories explain why firms may have incentives to redirect innovation, and recent evidence further shows that digitalization can facilitate green transformation; however, they say less about whether firms possess the internal knowledge conditions required to enact such redirection (Yang et al., 2025). The direction shift at the enterprise level is often constrained by internal mechanisms. Organizational conventions stabilize the choice set and solidify the path (Nelson and Winter, 1982), and absorptive capacity requires enterprises to have prior accumulation in the target knowledge domain Cohen and Levinthal (1990). The complementary evidence of IT further indicates that whether technology investment can be transformed into performance depends on whether organizational capabilities can match it (Bloom et al., 2012). When digital-intelligence transformation expands the external search space, enterprises will simultaneously strengthen two types of knowledge recombination. Exploratory recombination brings fragmented external knowledge into the enterprise, expands combination possibilities and pushes the burden of system integration back to the internal side; exploitative recombination deepens and reuses the existing knowledge base, reduces integration costs, but strengthens comparative advantages and lock-in. The change in the green direction depends on whether enterprises can transform external exploration into internally usable combinations, and whether the increment of exploration is sufficient to cover the lock-in brought by exploitation. The technical distance and breadth of the industrial network will change the external combination space, thereby changing the slope of this trade-off (Palit et al., 2022). Under the framework of structural change, whether enterprises can expand the knowledge boundary and realize the advantages of technological change depends on the matching of organizational boundaries and knowledge boundaries (Kapoor and Adner, 2012).

To test for the existence of direction change due to knowledge boundaries rearrangement, this paper uses a panel data set of Chinese listed firms to measure direction using green patent share and model the connection between digital-intelligence transformation and direction change. Furthermore, it divides the knowledge recombination into exploration and exploitation two mechanisms and examines their opposing effects on the direction equation. Regarding empirical identification, digital-intelligence transformation is about endogeneity of adopt and disclosure decision-making. To overcome this problem, we use a natural experiment, historically exogenous lightning-demand shock, leading to variations in early laid optical fiber lines across firms and develop an instrumental variable Campante et al., (2022) which can also rule out firms’ own proactive decision-making behavior via the interaction between historic lightning frequency and national growth in optical fiber deployment. We further exploit IT and knowledge channels to distinguish heterogeneity of firms constraints and hence examine distinct roles played by outward acquisition capacity and internal integration conditions (Liu et al., 2024). All these designs are used to prove the proposed KM mechanism, instead of over-emphasize the direct observability of firms internal knowledge routines.

Contributions of this paper are found at three levels. First, this paper adds to knowledge management literature by theorizing digital-intelligence transformation as digital knowledge infrastructure to reform the firms’ knowledge search, translation and recombination. Second, this paper writes the green directional switch as an internal competition between exploration and exploitation, illustrates why tacit collaborative bottlenecks are more pronounced with less external acquisition costs, and sets both knowledge-base structure and integration conditions in direction choice. Third, this paper extends directed technical change from external relative returns to reordering of internal knowledge system, explains why a digital-intelligence shock leads to systematically diverse directional outcomes across firms and develops a testable formulation of organizational boundary conditions to facilitate green innovation.

## 2. Research hypotheses

## 2.1 Baseline hypothesis

This paper defines green-oriented technological transformation as the reshaping of an enterprise’s technology portfolio toward a green-orientation of technological approach. The alteration of technological direction in digital-intelligence transformation comes from restructuring the conditions of knowledge search and knowledge integration. Digitalintelligence transformation may reduce the costs of obtaining codifiable knowledge from outside the enterprise, enrich the scope of knowledge sets an enterprise can acquire, connect and exchange, and, meanwhile, render system integration and tacit cooperation as major constraints of the innovation function. The green-oriented change in technologica direction is contingent on the comparison of the increment gained from the extension of the external opportunity set with the decrement induced by the bottleneck of interna integration.

So, the extension of the external opportunity set have different degree of influence on the green direction. Green technology is more dependent on the complementarity and combination of heterogeneous items and the stitching-in of cross-domain knowledge, and the knowledge base of green technology are more discreet with more comprehensive standards and limits (Shahzad et al., 2020). Hence, the knowledge base of the company cannot be easily converted for the directional shift, and the search, screening and matching of external green factors possess significant fixed costs without the support of digitalintelligence and fail to bring large number of possible combinations into R&D search space. Digital-intelligence transformation decreases the fixed costs and thus increases the probability for green factors to join the R&D knowledge pool, and a comprehensive margin extension of technological direction occurs. Here, the extension refers to addition of combinable sets and achievable pathways, not a slight improvement upon the current direction.

The integration bottleneck does not weaken into a speed issue; it corresponds to the intensive marginal losses during the direction shift process. The fragmentation of externa inputs increases the requirements for translation and coordination, and concentrates the pressure of system integration within the organization. The influx of external knowledge is not equivalent to an internal capability leap. Insufficient contextual translation will introduce noise and induce integration dilemmas, and algorithmic processing may also squeeze out empirical learning, making the constraints of tacit collaboration more decisive (Nielsen, 2025; Bucher et al., 2024; Tasselli et al., 2025; Sundaresan and Guler, 2025). Therefore, digital-intelligence transformation simultaneously brings green opportunity dividends in the extensive margin and integration losses in the intensive margin. Since the key constraint for the green direction shift lies in whether external elements can enter the search space, the directionality increment released by extensive marginal expansion can absorb and cover the losses caused by the integration bottleneck in the overall net effect:

H1. Digital-intelligence transformation promotes firms’ directed technical change toward green technologies.

## 2.2 External search expansion and exploratory knowledge recombination

After digital-intelligence transformation reduces the acquisition cost of external encodable knowledge, the technological search of enterprises undergoes extensive marginal expansion. The number of knowledge elements accessible to the R&D search space increases, and the feasible set of cross-domain splicing expands, while the intensity of exploratory knowledge recombination rises. Exploration differs from the deep use of existing knowledge bases; it corresponds to generating new elements and combinations, and is accompanied by higher combination uncertainty and a wider search radius (March, 1991; Katila and Ahuja, 2002; Yayavaram and Ahuja, 2008; Bereznoy et al., 2021).

The core mechanism is that whether the fixed costs at the entry end of knowledge access is reduced. Digital-intelligence transformation’s retrieval, matching and data interface enable the enterprise access to cross-industry and cross-regional knowledge components at the lower cost, so the possibility that the external fragmented knowledge enters into the R&D path increases, thus it becomes more easier for enterprises to select the combinable technical components within a larger candidate pool. Innovation in green building materials can show this broad marginal extension mechanism more apparently. New materials formula, carbon footprint calculation rule, low-carbon construction method and green certification system are all in the individual entities or knowledge sectors. Traditional R&D tends to be local performance optimization, but after these heterogeneity knowledge components are inserted into a search space with digital-intelligence system, the probability of an exploratory combination of material, process and standard is enhanced and a green direction candidate path is triggered.

Green innovation has a more pronounced recombinative and boundary-spanning characteristic. The technical route for it usually cross-crosses originally independent andorthogonal knowledge fields, like materials science, energy engineering and regulations. Local optimality is easier to get trapped if it only relies on neighbor search of existing internal knowledge; a turn in direction becomes more sensitive to outside heterogeneous inputs. Digital-intelligence transformation allows a larger range of margin expansion, which enriches a knowledge pool of cross-domain combinations, boosts up frequent testing, trial-and-error and iterations in enterprises’ green-related combinations, leading it to move from current orbit to a new green combinations domain. Accordingly, exploratory knowledge recombination is one of core driving mechanisms underlying digitalintelligence transformation to green technological transformation:

H2. Digital-intelligence transformation accelerates firms’ directed technical change toward green technologies through stronger exploratory knowledge recombination.

## 2.3 Learning transferability constraints and exploitative knowledge recombination

Digital-intelligence transformation expands the external search space while reducing the costs of retrieving, encoding and reusing existing knowledge, thereby increasing the marginal returns of the intensive path. Companies can more easily consolidate the encodable experiences of high-performing units into callable rule sets and rapidly spread them within the organizational network, thereby strengthening exploitative knowledge recombination centered on reuse, improvement and standardization (Li and Sandino, 2025). This change does not require the assumption that enterprise preferences have changed; it stems from the rearrangement of knowledge-processing costs and learning incentives.

Transferability of learning re-frames this rearrangement of relative cost as a directional locking substitution effect. Transferability of process-based learning is low, and knowledge in high-learning-rate industries is more cumulative on incumbent experience, hence they are less keen on cross-domain expansion, favoring entry into industries with an analogous learning framework (Ryu et al., 2022). A digitalization-intelligence transition causes the reuse cost of encodable knowledge to decline, while the responsibility of translating and integrating cross-domain experimental mistakes falls back onto the firm, thus enterprises receive a different set of higher relative knowledge-processing incentives. Exploitative deepening in the existing domain of knowledge becomes the least resistance direction, resources and attention will be concentrated on the reuse pathways where tangible return can be acquired faster, pushing aside cross-domain integration. System continuity of the current collaboration network will reinforce such lock-in effect, multiple embedding secures system stability, squeezing the channels for exploring novel objects Yu et al. (2025).

The reinforcement of exploitative knowledge recombination has a directional bias toward green-oriented technological change. The green direction shift relies on the recombination of cross-domain elements and system-level integration investment. Exploitative deepening locks innovation activities within the existing knowledge domain, making it easier to conduct incremental improvements along existing comparative advantages, thereby weakening the extent to which innovation combinations move toward the green end. Given that the deepening of the intensive margin continuously crowds out cross-domain integration, this paper proposes the following hypothesis:

H3. Stronger exploitative knowledge recombination slows firms’ directed technical change toward green technologies.

## 2.4 Knowledge-base conditions for architectural integration and the realization of directional effects

Digital-intelligence transformation broadens the scope of external searching and brings in more heterogeneity factors to the enterprise’s R&D candidates set, while pushing system integration and implicit collaboration inward. The exploration dividends introduced in the last two sections converge on the same constraint: can the heterogeneous external factors be turned into viable engineering interfaces and replicable process flows. It is the conditions of knowledge base supporting architecturally integrating capabilities that make the transformation feasible and allows enterprise to bring into use cross-department, crossprocess and cross-standard knowledge fragments to achieve a working system and build coherent working interfaces at R&D end, manufacturing end and market end without jeopardizing the original production system stability.

Knowledge-base conditions for architectural integration shape whether external search expansion can be transformed into a broad marginal increment in the green direction. The reconfiguration attribute of green innovation is stronger, with technical components coming from mutually orthogonal knowledge domains, and the combined value depends on interface design and system integration. Research on architectural innovation indicates that innovation performance not only depends on the accumulation of knowledge at the component level but also on the mastery and update of inter-component knowledge connections. Insufficient inter-component knowledge will cause the organization to lose integration efficiency in the face of architectural changes (Henderson and Clark, 1990). The knowledge-base perspective further emphasizes that the core function of an enterprise lies in integrating dispersed knowledge and forming coordinated action rules, and the integration condition determines whether knowledge input can be transformed into output (Grant, 1996; Mele et al., 2024). In case digital-intelligence transformation adds exogenous fragments to R&D space, stronger knowledge-base support for architecture integration results in reduced unit costs of translation, interface definition, process verification and inter-department alignment, an increased conversion rate for explorative recombining, and facilitates green candidate path promotion to the implementable level.

Knowledge-base conditions for architectural integration also change the extrusion intensity of exploitative deepening in the direction shift. After digital-intelligence transformation lowers the reuse cost of existing paths, resources and attention tend to flow to exploitative activities that can quickly realize returns. Stronger integration-supporting knowledge bases enhance the controllability and predictability of cross-domain integration, making crossdomain experimentation less likely to come at the cost of production system instability, thereby weakening the advantage of exploitative paths in relative returns. Absorptive capacity theory points out that the value realization of external knowledge depends on the existing knowledge base and internal transformation process of the enterprise. Without the absorption and transformation links, external knowledge is difficult to enter productionoriented organizational procedures (Cohen and Levinthal, 1990). In this study, knowledge diversity and knowledge breadth are treated as observable knowledge-base conditions that support, but do not fully capture, architectural integration. They reduce the risk of transforming external exploration into internal capabilities and alleviate the extrusion of exploitative deepening on cross-domain integration. Thus, the net effect of digitalintelligence transformation on green-oriented technological change is more likely to be realized in enterprises with stronger integration-supporting knowledge-base conditions, and the directional promotion effect is stronger:

H4. The positive effect of digital-intelligence transformation on firms’ directed technical change toward green technologies is stronger when firms possess stronger knowledge-base conditions for architectural integration.

## 3. Research design

## 3.1 Sample and data

The sample spans Chinese A-share listed firms from 2014 to 2023. The start year is pinned down by data constraints. Before 2014, annual reports contain too little digital-intelligencerelated language to support a reliable text-based measure, and the WIPO IPC Green Inventory had not yet been widely incorporated into Chinese patent records [1]. Continuous variables are winsorized at the 1st and 99th percentiles. The final panel contains 5,013 firms and 31,946 firm-year observations.

CSMAR provides firm financial statements, while CNRDS provides patent records. Green patents are identified using the WIPO IPC Green Inventory. The firm-level digital-intelligence index is constructed from annual report text following Liu et al. (2024). Instrument construction relies on historical lightning-frequency data from NASA’s LIS/OTD lightning climatology and annual fiber-rollout statistics reported by China’s Ministry of Industry and Information Technology [2].

## 3.2 Variables

3.2.1 Outcomes. GreenShare Computed as the ratio of a firm’s green patent applications to total patent applications in year t, GreenShare is multiplied by 100 to obtain percentage points. In line with share-based measures of directed technical change (Acemoglu et al., 2016), this construction abstracts from innovation scale and isolates innovation direction [3]. DTC and DTC\_inv Defined as the first difference of GreenShare, DTC captures year-to-year adjustment in innovation direction. Differencing requires consecutive-year observations, leaving 27,133 firm-year observations for DTC. The invention-only counterpart, DTC\_inv, is constructed analogously after restricting the patent set to invention patents.

3.2.2 Digital-intelligence transformation. DigiIntell index construction follows Liu et al. (2024). Annual report text is tokenized and matched to a pre-specified dictionary of digitalintelligence terms covering AI, big data, cloud computing, blockchain and the Internet of Things. The index equals the share of matched terms in total word count, transformed as ln(1 + share) [4]. This text-based index captures firms’ disclosed digital-intelligence orientation, including technological adoption, managerial attention and strategic emphasis on intelligent digital systems.

3.2.3 Mechanism variables. Patent citations are used to construct two recombination measures in the spirit of Katila and Ahuja (2002) and Yayavaram and Ahuja (2008). Exploratory recombination (EXPY) captures revealed exploratory knowledge recombination and is defined as the log count of technology-class pairings that are new in the firm’s citation set in year t. Exploitative recombination (EXPE) captures revealed exploitative knowledge recombination by counting how often the firm repeats a technology-class pairing it has used before; the log of this repetition count is EXPE [5]. Together, these measures provide an observable basis for examining how firms restructure knowledge combinations in patentable innovation outputs.

3.2.4 Knowledge-base conditions as moderators. Knowledge-base structure is characterized with two measures. Knowledge diversity (Div) is the log count of IPC subclasses covered by the firm’s cumulative patent stock (Ju et al., 2024). Knowledge breadth (Br) equals 1 − Herfindahl, where the Herfindahl index is computed from the distribution of the firm’s patents across IPC subclasses (Breschi et al., 2003). These two measures characterize the diversity and breadth of the knowledge base that conditions firms’ architectural integration and recombination potential.

3.2.5 Controls. Controls include firm size (Size), leverage (Lev), return on assets (ROA), operating cash flow (Cashflow), Tobin’s Q (TobinQ), listing age (ListAge) and CEO duality (Dual), following Duan et al. (2024) and Jarmooka et al. (2021)[6].

## 4. Empirical results

## 4.1 Descriptive statistics

Green innovation is rare in the sample. GreenShare averages 0.128 and its median is zero, implying that more than half of firm-year observations record no green patent applications (Table 1). Digital-intelligence transformation is highly uneven. DigiIntell has a mean of 1.081, a median of zero and a standard deviation of 1.354; identification is therefore driven primarily by cross-firm dispersion rather than common time-series movement (Table 1). Recombination activity leans more toward exploration than exploitation on average, with mean EXPY (1.152) exceeding mean EXPE (0.650) (Table 1).

## 4.2 Baseline regressions

4.2.1 OLS estimates. Baseline regressions relate green direction to digital intelligence with firm and year fixed effects. The point estimate on DigiIntell remains essentially unchanged at 0.007 when controls are added and when overall patenting intensity (lnTotalPat) is included, suggesting the association is not mechanically driven by innovation scale (Table 2). Given the low baseline level of green direction, the implied magnitude is meaningful: a one–standard deviation increase in DigiIntell (1.354) translates into a 0.0095 percentage-point rise in GreenShare, about 7.4% of the sample mean (0.128). Directional adjustment measures respond more strongly. DigiIntell predicts a 0.032 increase in DTC and a 0.081 increase in DTC\_inv, indicating that the directional response is concentrated in invention patents, where search and recombination constraints are more binding (Table 2).

## 4.4 Placebo test and dynamic effects

Figure 1 reports a permutation placebo based on 500 random assignments of DigiIntell. Placebo estimates concentrate around zero, while the baseline estimate lies far in the tail. Figure 2 adds contemporaneous DigiIntell and one- to three-year lags. The effect emerges with delay: contemporaneous DigiIntell has little predictive power, while lagged terms turn positive and grow with the lag length. This timing accords with the implementation cycle that links search to patentable outcomes. In the implementation of digital-intelligence transformation, enterprises must first implement data systems and digital interfaces, then achieve cross-domain knowledge searchability, translation of outside into internal R&D problems, experimental combination across disciplines, and finally solution into patents.

## 4.5 Mechanism evidence

The framework implies that digital intelligence strengthens both exploratory recombination (EXPY) and exploitative recombination (EXPE), but that the two modes have opposite directional consequences. Exploration relaxes path constraints by enabling search in unfamiliar technological subdomains. Exploitation deepens familiar combinations and can reinforce comparative advantage along incumbent trajectories.

The evidence supports a two-stage structure (Table 5). In the recombination equations, DigiIntell increases both EXPY (0.021) and EXPE (0.018), indicating broadly similar effects on the two learning modes. The divergence appears in the direction equation. When both channels enter the GreenShare regression, EXPY loads positively (0.015) while EXPE loads negatively (−0.006), and a Wald test rejects equality (F= 18.23). The implied indirect effect through exploration is 0.000315, compared with −0.000108 through exploitation, leaving a positive net indirect effect of 0.000207. Exploration contributes about three times the magnitude of exploitation’s drag. Digital intelligence therefore shifts direction toward green technologies not because it suppresses exploitation, but because exploration’s directional force dominates the lock-in created by exploitation.

A natural reading links this asymmetry to incumbent trajectories. Exploratory recombination mixes heterogeneous elements and is more likely to generate outputs that fall into environmental technology classes covered by the WIPO green list. Exploitative recombination deepens within the incumbent neighborhood. When historical accumulation is predominantly non-green, deepening reinforces a non-green direction, producing a negative loading for EXPE. The low incidence of green patenting is consistent with this mechanism.

This evidence extends canonical accounts in two directions. External directional incentives remain central in standard models of directed technical change (Acemoglu et al., 2012), but internal recombination structure can generate directional adjustment even when those incentives are imperfect. In an evolutionary view (Nelson and Winter, 1982), routines and reuse contribute to lock-in; here, digitalization activates both lock-in and break-through forces, and the net direction reflects their relative strength inside the firm.

## 4.7 Heterogeneity

The digital-intelligence effect concentrates where alternative search capacity is weak and where institutions raise the return to green direction.

Star inventors provide one substitute. In firms with star inventors, the marginal association between DigiIntell and green direction is close to zero (−0.001) and estimated imprecisely. ## 4.8 Further analyses

4.8.1 Nonlinear effects. It may not even be true that the linear relation holds when the level of digital-intelligence transformation becomes high. In fact, screening costs could actually increase for firms faced with more dispersed information and less precise match-ups. From a KM point of view, this means diminishing marginal returns to knowledge restructuring: Initial digital-intelligence adoption leads to more knowable and codifiable knowledge, while higher level of transformation might induce information overload and weaker relevance on additional knowledge inputs. Organizational adjustment to new knowledge may lag behind technological adoption, thereby nullifying additional search economies. Furthermore, the highly digitized firm might re-engineer well-performing rules along the incumbent trajectory, thus entrenching cognitive routines and learning traps and reducing exploration of the uncertain green territory.

An inverted-U relationship emerges in a quadratic specification. The linear term is positive (0.0126) and the quadratic term negative (−0.0018), with a turning point around 3.5 near the 95th percentile (Table 8). Most observations lie in the region where marginal effects remain positive but declining. Figure 4 shows that marginal effects fall with DigiIntell and become imprecise beyond the turning point, where the confidence interval includes zero. A conservative interpretation is diminishing marginal returns rather than a definitive reversal.

4.8.2 Moderated mediation. Moderation and mechanism can be linked by asking whether knowledge diversity amplifies the DigiIntell and EXPY relationship. The DigiIntell × Div interaction is positive in the EXPY regression (0.015) (Table 8). This suggests that knowledge diversity can amplify the incremental gains from exploratory reorganization resulting from the expansion of the external search space through digital and intelligent technologies, thereby driving innovation toward green technologies.

## 5. Conclusions and implications

## 5.1 Main findings

This paper measures the technological trajectory of firms by using the proportion of green patents, and presents the change in trajectory through its first-order difference. Through benchmarks estimation, the increase of digital intelligence level correlates positively with the transition tendency of technological innovation direction to green. DigiIntell increased by one standard deviation (1.354) by 0.0095 percentage points, which is 7.4% compared to the sample mean (0.128).

The mechanism tests show that there is a two-way effect of digital intelligence: the stimulation degree of exploratory and exploitative recombination are basically equal, but there is a directional difference in the direction equation: the impact of exploration is positive, while that of exploitation is negative. In short, the positive impact is nearly three times more than the negative impact. This structure leads to diverse effects: green dividend gain from digital-intelligence transition attenuates with enterprises’ offline knowledge search endowment; the marginal impact on firms with superstar inventors or channels of university−industry cooperation is insignificant; the marginal impact on enterprises with less alternative search channels is larger; realization of direction is institutionally dependent; environment-oriented cities gain about four times greater margin green dividends than nonenvironmentally oriented cities; the conversion rate is knowledge base structure dependent; an inverted U-shaped regime of knowledge breadth exists and extremely wide range may dilute depth. It also appears that marginal benefit from digital-intelligence transition reduces on high-end side, whose inflection point is around 3.5.

## 5.2 Theoretical implications

This paper makes several contributions to the field of KM by relating directed technological change with the reassignment of internal knowledge systems in enterprise. The reduced cost of obtaining codified knowledge externally caused by digital intelligence, together with implicitly collaboration and system integration being the stronger constraint for innovation function make the direction switch determined by net value of exploratory and exploitative recombination. This evidence offers non-price based micro-foundation for directional technological change, and further reduces the path dependency to a function of endogenous resource allocation. The stronger resource allocation caused by exploitative recombination could absorb attention and resources, and the weakening of the constraints of combination caused by exploratory recombination drive direction switch.

In addition, this paper demonstrated that the activated strength of exploratory and exploitative recombination by digital intelligence is nearly equivalent, while the deviation of direction is produced by directional diversion at a later stage. Exploratory recombination would broaden the feasible space of green combination, and exploitative recombination consolidate the resource allocation along with current comparative advantages and constantly exert extrusion upon cross-domain integration. Hence, digital-intelligence transformation is viewed as a kind of general-purpose search infrastructure rather than a green-oriented device intrinsically. The depth dimension of the breadth dimension is still a constraint when the range of digital intelligence’s search space expands.

## 5.3 Management and policy implications

The reduction in reuse cost of existing paths and enhanced resource absorption and attention acquisition capabilities through exploitative recombination by digital intelligence will result in a rise in the possibility of getting locked in. Thus, to achieve the green direction shifting, management emphasis should change from tool investment to interface governance so as to improve interface definition, interdepartmental validation and management on iterative cycles; through such management, external element will transfer from information to executable processes and standard interfaces. Organizations which are too broad in scope of knowledge may suffer from breadth dilution and search capability created by the transformation of digital intelligence should be used for the deep penetration of core technology. When the degree of digital-intelligence transformation reaches a high degree, the diminishing marginal returns make continuous investment on tools narrower, with the limit to be information filtering, knowledge screening and organizational adjustment.

It is important that the policy tools have coordinated ability supply and directional guidance. Data of key cities show that environmental regulations and demand side benefits both bring search ability to green scope, and they exhibit complementary relationship. For the actual implementation, the policy can focus more on supply of integrated public goods instead of subsidies in the search aspect, which includes the establishment of low-carbon material and process databases, supply of uniform standards for carbon accounting and factor databases, standards for interface compatibility and data interconnection in upstreamdownstream green certification, platform for green process concept validation and shared trial production lines, third-party certification services with consideration of quality and carbon footprint and risk-sharing tools during the trial stage. Digital infrastructure and support in digital intelligence aspect have significant incremental contribution for enterprises groups with lack of alternative search channels due to their higher external search fixed costs and wider marginal increment on breadth. For those enterprises which have comprehensive offline knowledge network, the policy should emphasize the improvement of verification systems, standards synchronization, institutional supply for interdepartmental cooperation rather than repeatedly provision of search end subsidies.

## 5.4 Limitations and future research

This paper’s digital-intelligence indicators are derived from annual reports, the measurement error of which is substantial. Future work could explore input data more directly related to the process. The measurement of digital intelligence will be pushed from disclosure proxies to R&D processes, supply chains and process verification micro-levels. Further research could differentiate between changes in the direction of technical transformation and improvements in its quality, and locate the tipping point for candidates to move toward concrete implementable paths.

Future research could further illuminate the internal processes involved. While this study presents a description of trade-offs between exploitation and exploration of recombination, how internal integration bottlenecks develop and are resolved is not yet directly measured. Observable indicators should be constructed around interface specifications, crossfunctional task coordination costs, validation periods and costs of trial-and-error failures. Combined with the analysis of research and development records, process change logs and inventor collaboration networks at project level, it could trace the evolution of implicit constraints imposed on tacit collaboration under governance interventions, to revea endogenous constraint evolution as the driver of goal-directed technological change.

[4.] The dictionary contains 371 terms spanning AI, big data, cloud computing, blockchain and the Internet of Things (Liu et al. 2024). Annual-report language can reflect both technological practice and managerial signaling.

[5.] Katila and Ahuja (2002) distinguish two knowledge-search modes in manufacturing innovation: exploration (creating new combinations) and exploitation (deepening existing ones). We adopt this operationalization and embed it in a directed green-change setting.

[6.] The control set is included to account for resources and financial conditions (Size, Lev, ROA, Cashflow), valuation-related incentives (TobinQ), life cycle stage (ListAge) and governance structure (Dual).
